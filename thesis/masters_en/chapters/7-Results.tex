\section{Ideal encoding and QHED versus Sobel}
\label{sec:res-e1}
\emph{Question: is the FRQI encoding lossless, and how closely does QHED track a classical Sobel edge map?} The answer is that encoding is exactly lossless in simulation, while QHED diverges from Sobel by design as the image grows. This section reports experiment~E1, driven by \srccode{scripts/frqi_qhed_sobel.py} with primary CSV \srccode{frqi_qhed_sobel_metrics.csv}; the end-to-end pipeline is \Cref{sec:pipe-e1,tab:pipe-ideal,fig:pipe-ideal}.

\Cref{tab:encoding_baseline} reports SSIM $=1$ between the FRQI reconstructions and the originals at every test size: the ideal encoding is lossless. QHED--Sobel SSIM, by contrast, falls to $\approx 0.16$ at $8\times 8$ and $\approx 0.01$ at $16\times 16$. This is expected, because QHED is a Walsh--Hadamard contrast pipeline rather than a Sobel clone~\cite{Yao2017QHED}.

\input{generated/results_table_encoding_baseline}

The QHED--Sobel gap has a simple reading: Sobel responds to local gradients, whereas the FWHT stage in QHED redistributes energy across Walsh functions before Sobel is applied in the spatial domain. As $N$ grows, that spectral reshaping drifts further from Sobel's inductive bias, so SSIM drops even though FRQI is lossless in the simulation sense~\cite{QiskitTextbookQuantumEdgeDetection}.

\textbf{Takeaway.} FRQI encoding is lossless (SSIM $=1$); the QHED--Sobel gap (down to $\approx 0.01$ at $16\times 16$) reflects QHED's Walsh--Hadamard bias, not an encoding error.

\section{Transpiled structural cost versus image size}
\label{sec:res-e2}
\emph{Question: for the same FRQI state, how do transpiled CX and depth compare between the naive and v-chain decompositions?} The answer is that v-chain spends a few ancilla qubits to cut the CX count by roughly an order of magnitude at $16\times 16$. This section reports experiment~E2, driven by \srccode{scripts/frqi_structural_resources.py} with primary CSV \srccode{frqi_structural_metrics.csv}; the pipeline is \Cref{sec:pipe-e2,tab:pipe-structural,fig:pipe-structural}.

\Cref{fig:cx-vs-n,tab:structural_resources} summarize the answer at optimization level~3. For $16\times 16$, v-chain preparation uses 16~qubits and 11\,663~CX gates, against a scaled naive estimate of 114\,176~CX from one transpiled \texttt{noancilla} slice (10~qubits before ancillas). With this result, the first question from \Cref{ch:introduction} is answered: \textbf{yes, the v-chain decomposition is significantly more efficient than the naive decomposition when it comes to the amount of controlled-NOT gates required}.

\section{Robustness under depolarizing noise}
\label{sec:res-e3}
\emph{Question: how quickly does reconstruction quality degrade as depolarizing noise increases, and is one decomposition uniformly more robust?} The answer is that fidelity, PSNR, and SSIM fall smoothly with noise scale, and the naive/v-chain ordering is not fixed. This section reports experiment~E3, driven by \srccode{scripts/noisy_frqi_sweep.py} with primary CSV \srccode{noisy_frqi_metrics.csv}; the pipeline is \Cref{sec:pipe-e3,tab:pipe-noisy,fig:pipe-noisy}.

\Cref{fig:noisy-4,fig:noisy-8,fig:noisy-16} plot fidelity, PSNR, and SSIM against depolarizing scale for the noisy FRQI sweep. When it comes to the experiment related to the image of size $16\times 16$, v-chain results are not present due to RAM constraints.

For $4\times 4$, both naive and v-chain curves are present, and their ordering can cross because the two decompositions accumulate noise differently across their entanglement patterns. It can be noted that for $8\times 8$, simulation time and memory requirements increase. Lastly, for $16\times 16$, only naive results are present.

\section{Edge metrics after noisy reconstruction}
\label{sec:res-e4}
\emph{Question: does the v-chain CX saving translate into better edge maps after noisy reconstruction?} The answer is no: \textbf{under this noise grid the median edge-SSIM difference favors naive, and the paired test is not significant}, answering the second question from \Cref{ch:introduction}. This section reports experiment~E4, driven by \srccode{scripts/noisy_recon_qhed_edge_metrics.py} with primary CSV \srccode{noisy_recon_qhed_edges.csv}; the pipeline is \Cref{sec:pipe-e4,tab:pipe-noisy-edges,fig:pipe-noisy-edges}.

The primary noisy endpoint is \textbf{edge SSIM}: it compares QHED applied to the noisy FRQI reconstruction against Sobel.

\Cref{tab:noisy_edges_excerpt} feeds the paired statistics consolidated in \Cref{tab:wilcoxon-export}: across the eight $(\mathrm{image},\mathrm{noise\_scale})$ cells with $\mathrm{noise\_scale}>0$, the edge-SSIM difference (vchain$-$naive) favors naive and is not significant, so the large CX saving does \textbf{not} buy a v-chain edge-quality advantage under this noise grid. Per-image plots under \path{outputs/} (for example \path{noisy_recon_qhed_edges_test_4x4.png} and \path{noisy_recon_qhed_edges_test_8x8.png}) complement the CSV when \texttt{noisy\_recon\_qhed\_edge\_metrics.py} generates them.

\section{Readout mitigation slice}
\label{sec:res-e5}
\emph{Question: is inverting the calibration matrix changing the debias readout on the synthetic confusion model?} The answer is yes: across ten seeds the mitigated $p_1$ shifts systematically towards the unbiased value. This section reports experiment~E5, driven by \srccode{scripts/readout_mitigation_shot_sweep.py} with primary CSV \srccode{readout_mitigation_shot_sweep.csv}; the pipeline is \Cref{sec:pipe-e5,tab:pipe-readout,fig:pipe-readout}.

\Cref{tab:readout_summary,fig:readout-mitigation} summarize ten seeds on \texttt{test\_4x4}. The mean raw $p_1\approx 0.515$ shifts to $\approx 0.499$ after mitigation (mean $\Delta\approx -0.015$); the paired per-seed $p\approx 0.002$ ($n_{\mathrm{seeds}}=10$) supports a systematic shift on this synthetic model.

\section{Statistics put together}
\label{sec:res-e6}
\emph{Question: taken together, what do the paired naive versus v-chain statistics say across all metrics?} The answer is that only fidelity reaches a small $p$, while the other metrics remain inconclusive, so there is no clear v-chain edge-quality advantage over naive.
