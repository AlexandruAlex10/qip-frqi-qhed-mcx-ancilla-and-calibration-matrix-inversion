\begin{center}
    \textbf{\fontsize{20pt}{24pt} \selectfont ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\vspace{24pt}

Quantum image processing could speed up tasks such as filtering and edge detection, but current devices in the era of Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) allow for only limited circuits, few qubits, and considerable noise. The Flexible Representation of Quantum Images (FRQI) stores each pixel intensity as a rotation angle on a single color qubit, selected by MCX (Multi-Controlled-NOT) gates reading an address register. A naive approach to preparing this state, using these gates without auxiliary qubits, generates deep circuits and involves a large number of CX (Controlled-NOT) gates after compilation.

This dissertation aims to answer the question: if only the way the MCX gates are constructed is changed, while the FRQI state remains unchanged, does the cost and noise robustness suffer or remain unchanged? Two synthesis modes are compared: an \emph{ancilla}-free (naive) decomposition and a reusable \emph{v-chain} pattern using a few auxiliary qubits. These methods are applied on grayscale test images of sizes $4\times 4$, $8\times 8$ and $16\times 16$ using the Qiskit Aer simulator. The final stage, Quantum Hadamard Edge Detection (QHED), evaluated against a classical Sobel reference, is held constant to ensure that any difference in results is exclusively from the FRQI state preparation circuit. Three categories of measurements are reported in this thesis: transpiled resources (number of qubits, circuit depth and number of CX gates), reconstruction quality under synthetic depolarizing noise (PSNR, SSIM and state fidelity) and edge map agreement (SSIM between QHED and Sobel). Additionally, a proof-of-concept study corrects the color qubit readout bias by inverting an empirical $2\times 2$ confusion matrix.

All experiments were performed exclusively on the simulator, with no runs on IBM hardware. Some results for the $16\times 16$ v-chain test image are not available due to insufficient RAM on the test system.
