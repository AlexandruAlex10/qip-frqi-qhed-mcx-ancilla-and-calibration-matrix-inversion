\section{Transpiled structural cost versus image size}
The structural study answers a resource question that precedes any claim about image quality: given the same FRQI state, how do transpiled CX and depth compare between naive and v-chain multi-controlled decompositions? \Cref{fig:cx-vs-n,fig:depth-vs-n,tab:structural_resources} summarize results at optimization level~3. At $16\times 16$, v-chain preparation uses 16~qubits and 11\,663~CX gates versus a scaled naive estimate of 114\,176~CX from one transpiled \texttt{noancilla} slice (10~qubits before ancillas). The gap illustrates why multi-controlled synthesis choices matter for NISQ feasibility even when the abstract FRQI state is unchanged~\cite{Geng2023ImprovedFRQI}.

The depth panel mirrors CX: v-chain increases qubit width but reorganizes entanglement so that the transpiler can schedule fewer sequential CX layers. This does \emph{not} imply lower error rates in absolute terms---wider circuits can accrue more idling error---but it does shift the dominant error mechanism from gate count toward other factors such as decoherence during ancilla routing~\cite{Preskill2018NISQ}.

\section{Ideal encoding and QHED versus Sobel}
\Cref{tab:encoding_baseline} shows SSIM $=1$ between FRQI reconstructions and originals for all test sizes. QHED--Sobel SSIM decreases to $\approx 0.16$ at $8\times 8$ and $\approx 0.01$ at $16\times 16$, which is expected because QHED is a Walsh--Hadamard contrast pipeline rather than a Sobel clone~\cite{Yao2017QHED}. The transpiled \texttt{initialize} columns in the same CSV are a synthesis reference for statevector preparation and should not be confused with the hand-structured circuits in \cref{tab:structural_resources}.

\input{generated/results_table_encoding_baseline}

Interpreting the QHED--Sobel gap: Sobel responds to local gradients, whereas the FWHT stage in QHED redistributes energy across Walsh functions before Sobel is applied in the spatial domain. As $N$ grows, the spectral reshaping moves farther from Sobel's inductive bias, so SSIM drops even though FRQI is lossless in the quantum simulation sense~\cite{QiskitTextbookQuantumEdgeDetection}.

\section{Robustness under depolarizing noise}
\Cref{fig:noisy-4,fig:noisy-8,fig:noisy-16} plot fidelity, PSNR, and SSIM versus depolarizing scale for the noisy FRQI sweep. At $16\times 16$, default runs may omit v-chain curves when density-matrix memory limits are hit without \texttt{--allow-heavy-dm}.

At $4\times 4$, both naive and v-chain curves are present; their ordering can cross because noise affects the entanglement pattern differently in the two decompositions. At $8\times 8$, wall-clock time and memory grow; at $16\times 16$, omitting v-chain is an engineering artifact, not a theoretical statement that v-chain is impossible---only that exact density-matrix evolution at 16~qubits is expensive~\cite{NielsenChuang2010}.

\section{Edge metrics after noisy reconstruction}
The primary noisy endpoint is \textbf{edge SSIM}. It compares QHED applied to the noisy FRQI reconstruction with Sobel on the clean original. The results are recorded in \path{outputs/noisy_recon_qhed_edges.csv}. The excerpt below covers \texttt{test\_4x4} and \texttt{test\_8x8} at $\mathrm{noise\_scale}>0$; the full pivot is archived under \path{thesis/generated/tables.md}.

\input{generated/results_table_noisy_edges_excerpt}

\Cref{tab:noisy_edges_excerpt} supports the paired statistics discussed next: across eight $(\mathrm{image},\mathrm{noise\_scale})$ cells with $\mathrm{noise\_scale}>0$, the median $\Delta$\,edge\_SSIM (vchain$-$naive) is negative. The Wilcoxon $p$-value is $\approx 0.078$, so there's \textbf{not} a real advantage to using a v-chain under noise in this case. Qualitative per-image plots under \path{outputs/} (e.g.\ \path{noisy_recon_qhed_edges_test_4x4.png}, \path{noisy_recon_qhed_edges_test_8x8.png}) complement the CSV when generated by \texttt{noisy\_recon\_qhed\_edge\_metrics.py}.

Edge SSIM is a stringent proxy: it couples FRQI noise, partial trace decoding, QHED nonlinearity, and Sobel reference mismatch. A CX reduction in preparation therefore does not map monotonically to edge SSIM gains---an important lesson for NISQ algorithm papers that report only gate counts~\cite{Geng2022HybridNISQEdge}.

\paragraph{Reading the table row by row.}
\texttt{edge\_ssim} is the headline endpoint for the thesis narrative; its median $\Delta$ is negative with $p\approx 0.078$, so there's no v-chain superiority for edge maps under this noise grid. \texttt{edge\_psnr} shows a negative median as well but with a larger $p$-value, consistent with high variance in decibel-domain edge scores when QHED and Sobel disagree structurally. Full-image \texttt{ssim} and \texttt{psnr} deltas are near zero with $p\gg 0.05$, indicating that naive and v-chain reconstructions remain comparably close to the clean original even while edge maps diverge---a useful reminder that global fidelity does not imply perceptual edge agreement. Finally, \texttt{fidelity} attains a small $p$-value because the abstract quantum states differ in how noise accumulates across entanglement patterns; nevertheless, that statistical signal does not overturn the inconclusive edge story we foreground in the conclusions.

For readout mitigation, the paired-per-seed median $\Delta$ (mitigated minus raw) is $\approx -0.0147$ with $n_{\mathrm{seeds}}=10$ and $p\approx 0.00195$. These seeds are independent re-draws of finite-shot noise for the same confusion matrix; they quantify estimator variance, not temporal hardware drift.

\section{Limitations and honest reporting}
Results omit spatial noise correlations, drift, and crosstalk beyond what the models encode. Scaled naive baselines extrapolate from one slice and may miss global optimizations. $16\times 16$ v-chain noisy and edge columns are often \texttt{NaN}. Statistical tests are exploratory with small $n$; any narrative favoring v-chain must stay conditional on resolution and noise scale.
