\section{Quantum image representations}
The flexible representation of quantum images (FRQI) encodes normalized intensities as rotation angles on a color qubit conditioned on a position register~\cite{Le2011FRQI}. NEQR and related models store intensities in basis states of additional qubits, trading higher qubit width for simpler angle addressing~\cite{Zhang2013NEQR,Yan2014QImageRepresentation}. Surveys and trend papers compare representations by qubit efficiency, preparation cost, and compatibility with arithmetic circuits~\cite{Su2020NewTrend,Amankwah2022QPIXL,Das2023HybridQuditRGB,Dutta2021QuantumMechanicsBased,Ruan2021OpportunitiesChallenges,Wang2022ReviewQIP,EbrahimpourKomleh2024QuantumComputingImageProcessing,Farooq2025SystematicReview}. Roadmap articles emphasize that preparation---not the abstract representation---often dominates resource budgets on NISQ devices~\cite{Iliyasu2013FRQIroadmap,Geng2023ImprovedFRQI}.

\section{Quantum edge detection}
Yao \textit{et al.} introduced QHED on FRQI-encoded images and demonstrated the pipeline experimentally~\cite{Yao2017QHED}. The Qiskit textbook notebook provides a pedagogical FRQI+QHED reference implementation~\cite{QiskitTextbookQuantumEdgeDetection}. Parallel lines of work adapt classical Sobel, Prewitt, and Kirsch masks to NEQR and related encodings~\cite{Zhang2015QSobel,Fan2019SobelNEQR,Zhou2019PrewittNEQR,Xu2020Kirsch,Chetia2021ImprovedSobelNEQR,Liu2022EightDirectionSobelNEQR}. Hybrid and fully quantumized edge proposals target NISQ constraints explicitly~\cite{Geng2022HybridNISQEdge,Shubha2024EdgeDetectionQuantumized}. Image-filtering primitives such as guided filtering have also been ported to quantum circuits~\cite{Mu2025GuidedImageFiltering}. This thesis keeps the \textbf{QHED operator fixed} and varies only FRQI preparation structure.

\section{NISQ challenges}
NISQ devices exhibit limited coherence time, gate infidelity, and readout bias. Hybrid edge detectors combine shallow quantum layers with classical post-processing to remain executable today~\cite{Geng2022HybridNISQEdge}. Improved FRQI studies on superconducting processors document layout and readout bottlenecks even when logical circuits are small~\cite{Geng2023ImprovedFRQI}. Our experimental design therefore reports transpiled CX/depth, depolarizing-noise sweeps, and an isolated readout-mitigation slice rather than claiming device-ready advantage factors.

\section{Multi-controlled gate synthesis}
Multi-controlled $X$ and uniformly controlled rotations are the expensive primitives inside pixel-wise FRQI preparation. Recent decomposition papers reduce CX depth using ancillas, QFT-based constructions, or specialized unitary synthesis~\cite{Vale2023MCSU2,Azevedo2024LowDepthMCG,Arsoski2024MCXQFT,Zindorf2024EfficientMCG}. Qiskit exposes several \texttt{MCXGate} modes; we compare \texttt{noancilla} (worst-case scaling per slice) with \texttt{v-chain} (reusing $m-2$ dirty ancillas). This is an engineering choice aligned with synthesis literature, not a new quantum image format.

\section{Readout error mitigation}
Readout errors are often modeled as a stochastic matrix mixing computational-basis outcomes. Inverting an empirically estimated confusion matrix (with regularization) yields mitigated outcome probabilities; the repository documents the $2\times 2$ color-qubit case and implements a shot-based demo. Broader mitigation families (zero-noise extrapolation, probabilistic error cancellation) are left for future work but motivate reporting raw and mitigated $p_1$ side by side.

\section{Positioning of this work}
Relative to representation surveys~\cite{Ruan2021OpportunitiesChallenges,Farooq2025SystematicReview} and NEQR edge variants~\cite{Fan2019SobelNEQR,Liu2022EightDirectionSobelNEQR}, we contribute a \textbf{paired structural and noisy-simulation study} of FRQI preparation only, with QHED held constant. Relative to improved-FRQI hardware papers~\cite{Geng2023ImprovedFRQI}, we provide open, simulator-first evidence at multiple resolutions with explicit resource tables and honest missing-data flags at $16\times 16$. The goal is reproducible NISQ-oriented engineering insight, not a new edge-detection algorithm.
